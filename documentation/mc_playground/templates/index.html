MCAA Playground

2 music tracks × 2 taste spectra (instrumentation: acoustic → electronic, tempo: ambient → driving beat) — the engine's joint Monte Carlo over epistemic (attribute) and preference (weight) uncertainty, ranked by HAI (Holistic Acceptability Index)

Your preferences

Both attributes are 0–100 positions on a taste spectrum: instrumentation (acoustic ↔ electronic) and tempo (ambient ↔ driving beat). A preference has two independent parts:

  • the spectrum slider — where on the continuum you want to be (amber line in the sample strips). The engine scores each track by how close its sampled value lands to this spot, falling off towards both poles (set_target in production).
  • the importance slider — how much the attribute matters. Weights are normalized shares across active attributes, so only their ratio counts (with a single active attribute the share is pinned at 100% and the slider goes inert).

Each attribute has a lifecycle state:

  • active spectrum position as set; the weight centers a Dirichlet whose spread is weight perturbation (the curve above the importance slider).
  • dormant spectrum position unknown — every simulation draws a latent one from Uniform(0, 100), integrating over every possible taste; the weight share is drawn Uniform(0, dormant budget).
  • excluded don't care — dropped from sampling and ranking entirely.

This is preference uncertainty: every simulation draws a different plausible weight vector (and, for dormant attributes, a different plausible taste) around what you stated.

Track attributes

Per product × attribute you set a mean and a confidence. Confidence maps to a sampling std with the pipeline's calibrated formula std = max(5, 22·√(1−conf)) on the 0–100 scale, and each simulation draws the value from Normal(mean, std) — the curve shows exactly that distribution.

This is epistemic uncertainty: how sure we are about where the track actually sits on each spectrum (think: inferred from audio analysis or reviews).

Final ranking — as the recommendation engine decides it

This panel runs the exact production path (assemble_decision): products are ordered by HAI, but an HAI gap within Monte Carlo noise (2·SE) is not trusted — the tie-break cascade then compares P(1st), expected rank, and the head-to-head margin, and if none separates them the result is a declared tie. The confidence score blends the winner's normalized HAI, its separation from the runner-up, and a penalty for dormant attributes.

Sampled attribute values — one dot per simulation

One strip per track × attribute. Each dot is one Monte Carlo draw of that attribute (epistemic uncertainty made visible); its horizontal position is the sampled spot on the taste spectrum, the vertical position is just jitter so the density is readable. The two attributes are drawn independently, which is why they get separate axes. Colour = the rank the track achieved in that simulation, which folds in the weight draw too: 1st 2nd. The white dashed line marks the track's stated mean; the amber line is your chosen spot on the spectrum (tracks score higher the closer their samples land to it). A wide strip = low confidence, and a strip whose colour flips along the spectrum is a track whose rank genuinely depends on where it really sits.

Sampled preference weights

One strip per attribute weight; each dot is that attribute's share in one draw of the weight vector (preference uncertainty), vertical position is just jitter. Wider strip = higher weight perturbation; the dashed line marks the mean sampled share. With two active attributes the weights sum to 1, so the two strips mirror each other around 0.5; a dormant attribute instead shows its Uniform(0, budget) share.

HAI per track

HAI = Holistic Acceptability Index: the track's average rank-payoff across simulations (β₁ for 1st > β₂ for 2nd), rewarding consistently good over winning big in some draws and losing the rest.

HAI is the mean over simulations of the ROC (rank-order-centroid) metaweight βrank earned in each simulation (β₁ > β₂, shown under the bars). Its variance is therefore fully described by the rank-acceptability split — how often the product landed 1st / 2nd — shown as the stacked bar. The whiskers are ±2 Monte Carlo standard errors of the HAI estimate (the same noise band the engine's tie test uses); “per-sample σ” is the std of the βrank draws themselves.

Head-to-head win probability

Across all simulations: how often did each product achieve the strictly better compromise score (Q) than the other? This is the engine's pairwise_win_prob — the same quantity its tie-break cascade consults when the HAI gap is within noise. A bar near 50/50 means the two products are genuinely interchangeable given current uncertainty; push a confidence slider up (or weight perturbation down) and watch it polarize.
